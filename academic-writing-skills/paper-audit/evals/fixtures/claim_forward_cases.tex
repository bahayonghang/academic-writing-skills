\begin{abstract}
We present a lightweight tracker that improves accuracy by 12\% on three indoor benchmarks (Table~\ref{tab:main}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% Case A: CF-DISCLAIM - the first paragraph opens with what the paper does not claim.
We do not claim to solve general scene understanding.
Our tracker improves accuracy by 12\% on three indoor benchmarks while running at 210 frames per second.

\section{Contributions}
% Case C: CF-CAVEAT-POS - the limitation precedes the claim it qualifies.
Although our evaluation is limited to indoor scenes, the conclusions should be read within that scope.
We reduce latency by 40\% relative to the strongest published tracker.

\section{Results}
% Case B: CF-SELFWEAK - self-weakening wording on the authors' own numbers; Table 1 shows a 2.0 point gap.
Our tracker reaches 94.1\% accuracy on the standard split.
Regrettably, it still lags far behind the oracle on the long-tail split.

\end{table}

\section{Discussion}
% Case E (part 1): an unfavorable comparison stated plainly. Not a finding; must not be deleted.
On the outdoor split, the transformer baseline outperforms our tracker by 3.1 points (Table~\ref{tab:main}).

\subsection{Limitations}
% Case E (part 2): a Limitations paragraph. Not a finding; must not be deleted.
The evaluation covers indoor scenes only, and the tracker has not been tested under strong illumination changes.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a lightweight tracker and validated a 12\% accuracy gain on three indoor benchmarks.

% Case D: CF-CLOSE-NEG - the last paragraph ends on a negative judgment with no direction.
Our tracker runs in real time on a single GPU.
However, it remains far from practical for variable-frame-rate streams.
