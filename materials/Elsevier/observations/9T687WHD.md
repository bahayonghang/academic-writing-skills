- abstract: However, insufficient experimental data often hinders ML models' ability to accurately characterize the gasification process.
- abstract: Therefore, this study proposed a novel hard-constrained physics-informed neural network (HC-PINN) to predict biomass gasification syngas components.
- abstract: The results show that the HC-PINN model demonstrates impressive performance in test sessions, achieving an R2 value greater than 0.95 for Case 1 (where the training and testing sets are randomly assigned) and over 0.90 for Case 2 (where training and testing sets do not contain the same biomass feedstock).
- introduction: With the steady increase in world energy demand and the growing apprehensions of climate change, a diversified and sustainable energy production structure has acquired immense significance.
- introduction: Therefore, this work proposes a hard-constrained physics-informed neural network (HC-PINN), which achieves enhanced interpretability and generalization capability in predicting syngas components (H2, CO, CO2, CH4, and N2) of biomass gasification.
- experiments: In this study, the HC-PINN model and six other ML models (RF, GBR, HGBR, SVM, ANN, and PINN) were trained using the collected experimental data and employed to predict the five syngas components of biomass gasification.
- experiments: Overall, the HC-PINN model showed excellent predictive performance in both cases and exhibited good generalization performance.
- conclusion: This study presents a novel HC-PINN method that employs neural network parameters as the medium of physical knowledge integration, ensuring the global consistency of model predictions and enabling concise model training.
- conclusion: The testing results show that HC-PINN exhibits remarkable predictive performance, with R2 exceeding 0.95 and 0.90 for Case 1 (where the training and testing sets are randomly assigned) and Case 2 (where training and testing sets do not contain the same biomass feedstock), respectively, and RMSE below 3 % and 4 %, respectively.
- conclusion: In summary, HC-PINN outperforms the other six ML models (RF, GBR, HGBR, SVM, ANN, and PINN) regarding predictive performance, interpretability, and generalizability.
